# How the corrections were made

In this note "we" and "ours" mean Bruce Hartzler and the AI agents he directs; "Logeion's" means the text of LSJLogeion as Helma Dik keeps it. LSJAI's corrections to the text of LSJ come from reading the Logeion text of each entry against a second digital copy and, in doubtful cases, the printed dictionary, by AI readers whose answers were checked by a second AI reader, and from an AI agent applying what the checks upheld with a tool that holds whatever it cannot place exactly. Bruce Hartzler directed the work and chose what to publish.

## The witnesses

- **Logeion** (Helma Dik, `LSJLogeion`): the Perseus LSJ as edited at Chicago, one entry a line, Perseus's ids (`orig_id`) and betacode keys kept. The copy read was LSJLogeion's commit 486e34a7 of 21 Nov 2025; the corrections were then carried onto her commit 5f4f476 of 29 Oct 2026.
- **Copy B** (so called here; the readers' briefs and the verdict column used another label): a second digital copy of the dictionary, plain text, 119,553 entries: the file `lsj.txt` of the Internet Archive item Lsj--LiddellScott (archive.org/details/Lsj--LiddellScott), posted anonymously on 8 Apr 2017, in the copy a colleague gave Bruce Hartzler in 2020, who had fixed 153 typos in 120 of its entries (his first copy differs from the archive's file in seven lines, his last in about 160); the file carries no colophon. The source was identified on 30 Oct 2026, after the readings. It is not an independent typing of the print: of 139 misspelt English words that Perseus's digitization had and Logeion corrected in 2024, it shares 50 (grones for groves, exlravagant, mositen, pulied) and has the right word in 81, so it descends from the same digitization, corrected further by hands unknown. It also follows the print more closely in places: it keeps hyphens as printed, keeps an accent together with a quantity mark, and makes each Hesychius gloss its own entry.
- **The printed dictionary and the 1968 Supplement**, page images, read in the source review where the disagreement turned on them.

Where the two copies disagree, at least one has mistyped the print, and the reading decides which. Where they agree, the comparison sees nothing: an error the two copies share, inherited from the digitization, is not found by this method. The corrections of the first pass (1,228 on 29 Oct 2026; 1,433 applied as of 2 Nov, after the printed page was read for every one) are therefore a floor, not the dictionary proofread.

## The comparison

The two texts of each entry were aligned and every differing span became an item: the Logeion reading, copy B's reading, and the entry around them. 13,645 items in 280 units were prepared on 23 to 25 Oct 2026; 6,856 of them were read over four rounds (the last 489 from a preparation refreshed on 29 Oct), 6,789 were not. Sixteen kinds of systematic difference between the two copies (quantity marks, hyphenation and the like) were ruled on as classes, not read item by item.

## The readers

The reading was run as a nightshift by Lychnos, an AI operator role, on 25 to 29 Oct 2026:

- a primary reader on each item: OpenAI's Sol 6.1 (`gpt-6.1-sol`) at its highest reasoning effort, through Codex (`codex exec`), except on 1,194 items in 25 units (W-038 to W-065) resumed on 28 Oct, whose primary reader was Astra (`gpt-6-astra`, ultra effort), through the same Codex runner;
- a blind check on a fixed share of the items (about a fifth) by a second reader: Astra (`gpt-6-astra`, ultra, through Codex), and on 152 of the resumed items Sol 6.1 (`gpt-6.1-sol`); where an Astra check of a resumed unit had been made before the resume it was kept, so there the check and the primary are the same model; the checks' answers were reconciled with the primary's;
- a source review of the disagreements by the second reader, against the printed dictionary and the Supplement where page images were to hand;
- a root acceptance of every delivery: the payloads' hashes, the validators, the external pins replayed.

Each item's verdict is one of `B` (copy B has the print), `Current` (Logeion has the print), `same` (the difference is no difference), `both-wrong` (with the text the print has), or `unknown` (held, with the reason). Every verdict carries a certainty, a reason, and where it can, the evidence quoted with its source.

## The application

A correction is applied by Aristarchus, the project's AI philology agent, with a tool (`tools/apply.py`) that finds the item's span in the entry's XML line by aligning the preparation's plain text with the line's own text, tags dropped and entities read, and replaces exactly that span. It holds, and lists, a correction whose line is no longer the one the preparation read, whose span crosses a tag or a sense boundary, which overlaps another span on the line, or which would leave the line other than well-formed XML. Copy B's literal `<` and `>` go in as entities. An inserted span goes inside the element whose word or number it continues, keeps copy B's space beside it, and sets punctuation against the word it closes or opens; a corrected number in a citation remakes the citation's `n` the way it read from the old number, and a change whose `n` cannot be derived so is held. These rules were added on 31 Oct 2026, after the first application had placed some spans wrongly; its `--repair` pass mended those in place, and the citations whose `n` it could not derive are listed in `corrections/bibl-n-held.tsv`. Keys, `id`s and `orig_id`s are never changed: an accent corrected in a head is not yet in Logeion's betacode key for it, and the key is what Logeion and Perseus look an entry up by.

A head's visible text in Logeion is its `orth_orig` without hyphens, quantity marks, supplement brackets and a closing ano teleia (114,156 of 116,930 heads follow this). Where a correction changed `orth_orig` and the text had read as the printed form before, the text was made from the corrected form the same way (37 heads); where it had not, it was left and listed (48).

The 81 held corrections are in `corrections/held.tsv` with their reasons; the readers' verdicts on them stand, the application waits for a hand.

## Carrying the corrections onto LSJLogeion's HEAD

Our corrections were made on the 21 Nov 2025 copy. Helma Dik changed 15,841 entries between that copy and 29 Oct 2026 and added 237, mostly citation markup, spacing and entry splits. Each entry we changed was carried onto her current line by a three-way comparison (`merge.py`): where Logeion's line was unchanged, our corrected line replaced it ("replaced Logeion's" in the tables); where Logeion had changed the entry elsewhere, our corrected spans were carried onto its line ("carried onto Logeion's"); where a Logeion change and ours touch the same place (21 entries), Logeion's line stands and ours is listed in `corrections/merge-report.tsv` ("Logeion's kept"); 10 entries Logeion had already corrected the same way. So 1,132 of the 1,163 entries we corrected are changed here. Every file was parsed whole afterwards.

## The citations marked by rule

LSJ's markup holds 455,349 citations (`<bibl>`), and a census of 31 Oct 2026 found about 63,000 more written in the text outside it, most of them inscriptions and papyri cited by their corpus, Galen by Kühn's volume and page, and lexica by the word. `tools/mark_citations.py` marks them, adding tags and never touching the text: a name the markup tags as an author or a title followed by a number (`IG 2(2).956.58`, `Phot. p.3`), a bare number after a citation (`Il. 14.271, 300`), an `ib.` or `l.c.` with a number, and the 5,132 spans an AI reader ruled citations on 31 Oct 2026 (a name standing alone, `Hsch.` cited by the word; a dotted number). A new citation carries a Perseus reference only where the dictionary's own citations make one by rule: a name whose citations name one work, the number in that work's numbering, a bare number continuing the work before it; elsewhere it has none until its address is known. A citation quoted through another (`Herophil. ap. Placit. 4.5.4`) is marked on its source after the `ap.`, the quoted author outside, as the dictionary's own citations are. Parts of a citation already marked (a face letter, `Def. 2`), second addresses, and what crosses an element are left, and listed. The 317 spans the rule could not settle were read again by AI readers the same night: of the 296 they ruled citations, 262 are marked by the text the reader read as the citation, with 7 bare numbers that continue them, and 34 are held and listed with the reason (`corrections/residue-c.tsv`); where the rule had marked a part of one (`Nic. Dam. Fr. 127 J.`, the rule's `Dam. Fr. 127`), its label was widened to the reader's, 7 of them, 8 of the rule's labels gone. Where the `<cit>` of the quotation before such a citation closed inside it (`IG 11(2).161</cit> B 38`), the `</cit>` was moved to after the citation, and where a `<foreign>` held the stop after one, the stop was moved out of it, as the dictionary's markup has it elsewhere, the text unchanged byte for byte; the moves, 204 for these and 4 for those below, are listed in `corrections/markup.tsv`. A second pass marks the names that cite a place by its word: a lexicon closing a gloss (`Hsch.`, `Suid.`, `Phot.`, `Gloss.`), with its `s.v.` and the word where they are printed, 21,108 of them, and the 269 second addresses the reader ruled so (`Fr. 75.2 Pf.`, the content of its bracket), each its own citation with no reference; it widens 471 of the first pass's citations to the author printed before them, as the reader's reference has it (`Apollon. Lex. s.v. γραπτῦς`). It leaves a name quoted through the citation after it (`Apollon. ap. Orib.`) and a name for its usage (`freq. in Gloss.`), and lists for a reader a work cited by page standing alone (`Eust.`, `Poll.`) and the second addresses no reader has read. Read by AI readers on 1 Nov 2026, 129 of those 221 are a citation (31) or a second address (98): 126 are marked by the reader's printed text, a second address as its own citation, and 3 stand marked already on their source after `in` or `post`, as the dictionary's own citations do (`Choerob. Orth. in AB 1380`). Each new citation is numbered after the entry's own, so the readings, the previews, and the verdicts keep their numbers. A check compares every entry's text before and after, byte for byte.

## The readings of the citations and the glosses

`readings/lsj-readings.txt` is a different kind of work: every citation in LSJ's entries read against the texts cited, in the project's digital corpora of Greek and Latin, by AI readers (Anthropic's and OpenAI's models) from 8 Oct to 2 Nov 2026, 200 of the rows by hand; the rows were re-keyed to this copy's text where Logeion's edits moved a citation (101 moved, 28 taken out whose citation is gone or now points elsewhere). A row names the entry (`orig_id`) and the citation's number within the entry, the verdict (`verified`: the cited line holds the word; `repaired`: the word is at the line given instead; `scholion`: the entry cites a scholion or a variant at that line; `relocated`: the citation belongs to another work; `unresolved`), the line as a CTS URN in the canonical numbering of the corpora, who read it, when, how sure, why, and the SHA-256 of the LSJ copy it was read on. `glosses/lsj-glosses.tsv` gives 2,481 entries a short meaning chosen from the entry's own definitions, read and checked the same way.

## The text cited and its apparatus

Since 2 Nov 2026 the page shows one text under each citation, with its witnesses. The witnesses are every edition the project holds that has the passage: the open texts of the Perseus Digital Library and the First Thousand Years of Greek, DigiLibLT, the Thesaurus Linguae Graecae's texts, the Loeb Classical Library, the Duke Databank of Documentary Papyri through the Packard Humanities Institute, PHI's Greek Inscriptions, and PHI's Latin texts. The cut (`tools/snippets.py`) finds every edition's lines. The collation (`tools/collate.py`) runs once after every cut and joins them into one record per citation, `docs/snippets/text`, which this repository carries.

The base is an open edition wherever one has the passage. The text shown is the base's text, word for word. There is no vote across witnesses: another edition's reading never enters the text; it stands in the apparatus. The witnesses are compared word by word. Before the comparison, differences that no edition means as a reading are set aside: the form of the apostrophe, Unicode composition, oxia and tonos, iota subscript and adscript, punctuation, capitals, and a word split at the end of a line with its tail at the start of the next. A witness with the same line numbers is compared line by line, edges included. A witness that gives a sentence or a section (Perseus's prose, the Loeb's) is aligned to the line inside its text by the longest common subsequence of the words' keys, edges ignored. A witness that shares fewer than half the words of the line is not aligned there and is only named. The apparatus lists, at each word, the witnesses that read otherwise (`ἀΐσσονται] ἀίσσονται Loeb`), and the runs where a witness has more or fewer words. A run longer than six words is given as a count, so that the apparatus stays the size of a quotation.

The counts on the last cut of 2 Nov 2026: 455,107 citations collated; 344,531 with two witnesses or more; 131,258 with a variant; 58,717 witness lines not aligned. The tool's `--report` lists, for each edition, how often it was a witness, how often it could not be aligned, and how often it differs. The editions that fail most are lyric texts whose editors divide the lines differently (Pindar, Aeschylus, Sophocles, Aristophanes) and Aristotle, cited by Bekker page and line against editions divided into chapters. That list is the work for the next pass over how those texts are generated. Not yet counted: how many variants are diacritics alone rather than different words. A majority of witnesses is a candidate reading, not proof, since several digitizations may descend from one edition; that is why the text takes no vote.
